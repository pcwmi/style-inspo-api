## Key Emotional Insight

**"I don't want to see my own picture."**

Rana shared that in her head, she's still in her 30s. When she sees photos of herself now (in her 50s), she doesn't like it. When Pei-Chin mentioned "we'd like to see a hotter version of ourselves," Rana strongly agreed — she doesn't want to see her actual photo.

### What This Reveals

The self-image gap:
- **Mental self-image**: Younger, more vibrant version
- **Photo reality**: Triggers disappointment, avoidance
- **Response**: "I don't want to see my own picture"

### Product Implications

Pei-Chin's reflection:
> "Is what I'm building to inspire or to actually wear it and feel good? The goal is the latter, but when someone doesn't love how they look, maybe this app can help them find the right outfit, but it's just a lot more complicated problem."

**Tensions:**
1. **Inspire vs. Practical** — Aspiration creates desire; reality can deflate it
2. **Visualization paradox** — Try-on is compelling, but showing someone's actual body might trigger the self-image gap
3. **Confidence as outcome** — The goal is "feel good wearing it," but that requires bridging internal self-image with external reality

**Design implications:**
- The "relatable model" (not personal photo) approach validated even more strongly
- For users with self-image gaps, showing a similar-but-aspirational model may be *kinder* than literal accuracy
- The app might help with *what* to wear, but can't solve the deeper "I don't like how I look" problem
